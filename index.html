When should a firm stop producing?

A firm that takes the market price as given keeps producing in the short run as long as the price covers its average variable cost. Drag the price line on the chart, or use the sliders, to see where that stops being true.

Shutdown point

This is where marginal cost crosses the lowest point of average variable cost.1 2 3 At any price below it, revenue cannot cover variable costs, so the firm loses less by closing.2

Break-even point

At the lowest point of average total cost, price equals ATC and the firm earns a normal profit.3 Between the two prices the firm makes a loss, but it still covers its variable costs and part of its fixed costs, so it keeps producing in the short run.2 3

Supply curve

For a firm in perfect competition, the marginal cost curve from the lowest point of average variable cost upward is the firm's supply curve.2 That is the thick line on the chart.

The numbers come from a worked example. Variable cost is Q³ − 6Q² + 14Q, so AVC = Q² − 6Q + 14 and MC = 3Q² − 12Q + 14. The lowest AVC is $5.00 at 3 units. Fixed cost comes from the slider, and the firm is assumed to be unable to recover any of it by closing. If some fixed costs can be avoided by closing, the cutoff price is higher.1
